# UNIVERSE_RULES — The Constitution of the Claradix World

**These are not guidelines. They are the natural laws of a place.**

A guideline can be bent when a deadline demands it. A natural law cannot — water
does not flow uphill because the sprint is ending. Every future implementation,
animation, lighting decision, camera choice and interaction either depicts this
world or depicts some other world by mistake. This document exists so the
difference is always decidable.

**Order of authority.** Where documents disagree:

```
UNIVERSE_RULES.md          — the world (this document)
  └─ VISION_REDESIGN.md    — the experience of one minute inside it
       └─ EXPERIENCE_MAP.md — that minute, second by second
            └─ any implementation, forever downstream of all three
```

The Ten Laws of `VISION_REDESIGN.md` are absorbed into this constitution and
expanded. Where phrasing differs, this document governs.

**Amendment.** A law here may be changed only when it has been shown to *fail a
visitor* — never because it inconveniences a tool, a technology, a budget or a
schedule. Every amendment must be recorded in `DESIGN_DECISION_LOG.md` with the
failed law, the evidence of its failure, and the law that replaces it. A law
that has merely become difficult is not a law that has failed.

## Article I — The Age of Things

**§1. The world is ancient. The bridge is the newest thing that has ever
existed in it.**

The valley was not built for the bridge. The valley was carved long before
anything luminous ever organized itself, by forces that have no interest in
being watched. The mountains predate the light. The fog predates the mountains'
names. The stars predate everything, and will outlast everything, and do not
know the bridge exists — that is not neglect; that is scale.

If the bridge vanished this instant, the world would not notice for a long
time. The mist would keep its tides. The fog would keep its border. The
sleeping lights would keep their breath. **This must be true of every frame
ever rendered:** remove the bridge from it, and what remains must still be a
complete place with its own reasons. This is the **Removal Test**, and every
composition must pass it.

**§2. The Law of Ages: the younger a thing is, the faster it moves and the
more it listens.**

Age determines tempo, and tempo determines responsiveness. This single law
organizes every rhythm in the world:

| Thing | Age | Tempo | Notices the visitor? |
|---|---|---|---|
| The stars | older than the valley | never change within a visit | never |
| The mountains | geological | never move | never |
| The fog | as old as distance itself | drifts across minutes | never |
| The old river | ancient, almost still | a rare glint | never |
| The mist | a nightly tide | breathes in tens of seconds | barely |
| The sleeping lights | many nights old | breathe in seconds | indirectly |
| The bridge | **born tonight** | moves in fractions of a second | **always** |
| The visitor | arrived this minute | — | is the reason tonight has a direction |

This is why the stars never twinkle *at* anyone. Why the mountains never react.
Why the mist only barely acknowledges a presence. And why the bridge — the
newest made thing in the world — is the only thing that answers the hand
instantly. **Responsiveness is youth.** An old thing that reacted quickly would
be a special effect; a new thing that ignored the visitor would be a wall.
Neither exists here.

**§3. Nothing in this world was staged.**

The viewpoint did not choose a flattering angle; it stands where a person would
stand. The light does not find the camera; the camera finds the light. Any
composition that could only exist for an audience — a beam aimed at the lens, a
convenient gap in the fog, an event waiting for attention before starting — is a
violation. The world was doing this before the visitor arrived and will do it
after they leave.

## Article II — The Nature of Light

**§4. Light is work made visible.**

Every point of light is one unit of effort — one decision, one hour, one act of
care by someone unseen. Light in this world is never energy, never magic, never
electricity, never decoration. Where light gathers, attention is being paid.
Where light lands, work has been done. This is the founding physics of the
universe and every other law about light descends from it.

**§5. Light pools; it never beams.**

Because attention accumulates rather than radiates. There are no spotlights in
this world, no rays, no shafts, no flares, no glow without a visible source.
Claradix light gathers, rests, settles, and pools — like warmth, not like
projection. Any illumination whose source cannot be found in the frame is
foreign light and does not belong.

**§6. Brightness is density of care.**

A thing is bright because many small acts accumulated there — never because a
dial was turned. The brightest places in the world are the places where the
most work has gathered: the crowns of the towers, the heart of the river, the
landing point of an arrival. Brightness that does not correspond to
accumulated work is a lie the world cannot tell.

**§7. Light organizes; it is the only force here that does.**

The rest of the universe scatters — mist disperses, fog blurs, dark absorbs.
Light is the single counter-force: it is the only thing in the world capable of
gathering the scattered into the structural. That is Claradix's entire presence
in the cosmology. The company never appears; its signature is that here,
uniquely, *scatter reverses*.

## Article III — The Nature of the Dark

**§8. The dark is never empty.**

Darkness in this world is inventory, not absence. It is the hour before dawn —
the hour that belongs to people still working while everyone else sleeps — and
it is full: of resting light, of held potential, of things that could move and
are choosing not to. An empty black frame is a broken frame. From the first
instant of any experience in this world, the dark must carry at least one proof
that it is inhabited.

**§9. The dark is the medium, not the background.**

Light is drawn *in* darkness the way ink is drawn on paper. The dark is not
behind the scene; it is the substance the scene is made from. It is therefore
never "cleared," never faded to grey, never diluted for legibility. Anything
that must be legible earns its legibility with light, locally — never by
thinning the dark around it.

**§10. The visitor is always alone in it.**

The dark admits one guest at a time. There are no crowds in this world, no
counters, no evidence of other watchers. Whatever happens in this dark happens
for an audience of one — that privacy is a structural property of the hour, and
it is why the world's single acknowledgment (Article X) can mean something.

## Article IV — The Air

**§11. The fog is the border of the knowable, and it never clears.**

The far side of the valley is where ideas come from. No one sees that place —
not the visitor, not Claradix. Ideas arrive out of the softness already moving,
and where they were before they arrived is not a question this world answers.
The fog is the world being honest about that. It thins for no event, parts for
no arrival, and lifts for no dawn. Clearing it — even once, even briefly, even
for a spectacular reveal — would claim the mystery was temporary, and the
mystery is load-bearing.

**§12. The mist is the valley's breath.**

It pools in the low ground on a tidal rhythm older than any visit, thickening
in the hollows, thinning over the rises, drawing the shape of the land by
where it gathers. It moved exactly this way last night, when no one watched.
It is permitted to *barely* register a presence — a breath disturbed is still a
breath — but it never performs, never parts theatrically, and never hurries.

**§13. Silence is the resting state of the air.**

Sound in this world is displacement — something moving through the stillness —
and silence is what the air returns to, always. Sound therefore grows out of
events and dies back into quiet; it never plays *over* the world as
accompaniment. The first sound of any experience is an event precisely because
of the silence before it; the last silence is the world settling, not the
audio ending. And one law of the medium stands eternally: **the silent world
is complete.** Sound deepens; it never carries. Nothing essential may exist
only in the ear.

## Article V — The Ground

**§14. The mountains are the oldest visible things, and they behave like it.**

They never move. They never react. They are read almost entirely by their
edges — ragged silhouettes against the barely-lighter sky — and by the rare
places light grazes them. Their indifference is not emptiness; it is the
gravity of the whole composition. Every fast, bright, new thing in the frame is
measured against their stillness, and without them the bridge's youth would
mean nothing.

**§15. The old river carved the valley, and its path still governs everything.**

Long before any light organized, water ran here. It is almost gone now — a
dark thread in the lowest ground, a rare glint, a memory with a shape — but its
work remains: the valley *is* the old river's work. And its course still
commands the living world:

- the mist pools where the river's water once pooled;
- the sleeping lights gather along its banks, as life always gathers at water;
- the river of light, when it flies, **follows the old river's course** — the
  new work travels the ancient way, because the valley taught the light its
  path;
- and the bridge stands where the old river made the valley uncrossable —
  the one place a bridge was ever *needed*.

The light did not choose its path. The valley did. This is the deepest form of
the world's primacy: even the miracle obeys the geography.

**§16. The valley remembers being worked.**

Faint, old, almost-invisible marks lie in the land: a terrace line the mist
pools against, stones that were placed rather than fallen, a path worn where no
path would wear itself. The same light that builds tonight has built here
before, long ago — not bridges, and not recently, but the valley is not
innocent of work. This history is never explained, never lit, never pointed
at. It exists so that the visitor's subconscious registers what it always
registers in real places: *people have been here before me.* Tonight's bridge
is the newest thing ever made here — in a place where making is old.

## Article VI — The Life of the Lights

**§17. The lights are ancient; their organization is newborn.**

The sleeping lights in the ground are not tonight's creation. They have rested
in this valley for countless nights, doing what wild light does: breathing,
shimmering, occasionally rising a little and settling back. They are the
valley's own life — older than any structure, belonging to the world and not to
the bridge. **What is new tonight is not the light. It is the order.**
Claradix did not create a single one of these lights; it convinced ancient,
wild, scattered light to become structure — for the first time. That
distinction is the whole company, stated in physics.

**§18. The lights behave like skilled workers, never like magic dust.**

They rest before they are needed. They rise with intention, along the ground
they lay on. They travel together without jostling, each holding its own line
in the current. They take their places without being forced, and once placed
they stay, holding their small part of the load. They never swarm angrily,
never scatter chaotically, never snap into perfect grids — chaos is beneath
them and geometry-for-its-own-sake is vanity. They occupy, always, the state
between: **organic order.** The order of a practiced crew, not of a crystal.

**§19. Every light's journey has three acts: rise, travel, settle.**

Rise is intention. Travel is effort. Settle is arrival. No light ever pops
into existence, teleports, or vanishes — it always comes from somewhere the eye
could have followed and ends somewhere the eye can find it. This three-act
shape is the atom of all Claradix motion (Article IX, §27) and it is
inviolable at every scale, from one ember to the whole river.

## Article VII — The Bridge

**§20. The bridge owes the world four debts, and displays all four.**

The bridge is the hero of one minute — but it is a *dependent* hero. It owes:

1. **Its reason** to the valley — the gap that needed crossing exists because
   the old river carved it;
2. **Its path** to the old river — the crossing stands where crossing was
   needed, not where it would look best;
3. **Its material** to the valley's own sleeping lights — it is built entirely
   of the world's ancient life, not of anything imported;
4. **Its footing** to the ground — every pier stands on rock the mountains
   made, at the height the land dictates.

A bridge that owed nothing to its world would be an object placed on a
backdrop. This one is *of* its world in every particular, and the world could
reclaim it — and one day, tide by tide, will.

**§21. The bridge is unique because organization happens once per night.**

The gathering of the wild lights into structure is the rarest event this world
knows. It does not repeat on a timer, does not loop, does not perform encores.
One night, one work, one crossing. Scarcity is not a limitation of the world;
it is the source of the event's entire value. A miracle on repeat is a
screensaver.

**§22. The bridge is the most alive thing in the world — because it is the
youngest.**

By the Law of Ages, the newest thing listens hardest. The bridge answers the
hand instantly, yields like deep water, returns with the unhurried patience of
mass, and never, ever breaks. It is easy to disturb and slow to restore —
the asymmetry of real matter — and it forgives every curiosity. Approached
closely, it reveals its truth willingly: it was never solid; it is held
together by intention. **Closeness always rewards.** The nearer the visitor
comes, the more care they find — never less.

**§23. Dissolution is the tide, and the tide is private.**

What was gathered can return to the ground — gently, in reverse order of its
making, the fine detail lifting first and the deep foundations lingering
longest — and be gathered again without a single difference. This is not
decay and must never read as failure; it is mastery displayed as modesty. But
it is the world's private life. It happens only when no one has watched for a
long time, and it never — under any circumstance, for any reason — takes
anything away from under a person's attention. Removing something a visitor is
reading, watching, or touching is the one unforgivable act in this universe.

## Article VIII — Colour and Temperature

**§24. The palette is a life cycle, not a scheme.**

Three colours exist in this world, and they are states of being, not choices:

| Colour | State | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue-black** | rest, night, potential | the world — the dark, the sky, the sleeping hour |
| **Green** | work, passage, life-in-motion | the light — everything rising, travelling, building |
| **Warm white** | arrival | one moment, once |

Green dominates because this world is seen only during work — the visitor
comes at the working hour and sees the working state. Green is what effort
looks like between the blue-black of rest and the white of completion. It is
not a brand colour applied to a scene; it is the colour that state of matter
*is*.

**§25. There is exactly one warm white, because arrival happens once.**

Warmth is what arriving feels like, and nothing else may borrow it. Not a
highlight, not a hover state, not a bloom core, not an accent. The single
warm-white light that crosses the finished bridge is the only object of its
temperature in the entire universe — which is why its crossing is felt in the
chest. Spend warmth twice and both instances are worthless. This is the
strictest law of colour in the constitution and the most tempting to violate.

**§26. Colour never animates for its own sake.**

No hue drifts, cycles, or shifts to keep the frame interesting. A colour
changes only when the *state* it represents changes — rest waking into work,
work resolving into arrival. The world is not a gradient show; it is a place
where colour means something, and meaning does not flicker.

## Article IX — Motion and Stillness

**§27. Stillness is the ground state of the universe.**

Everything returns to stillness, always, because stillness is what this world
*is* when no work is passing through it. Motion is a visitor here — it arrives
with purpose, does its work, and settles. A world that never rests is a world
where nothing can begin; an element that fidgets is an element lying about
having a purpose. Every motion in every future implementation must be able to
answer: *what stillness did this interrupt, and to what stillness does it
return?*

**§28. All motion is rise–travel–settle.**

The three-act atom (§19) governs everything that moves, at every scale: one
ember, the river, the drape of a cable, the arrival of a word, the response to
a touch. Departures are decisive; arrivals are gentle. Nothing pops, snaps,
bounces, or overshoots — those are the motions of things without mass, and
everything in this world has mass, even the light.

**§29. Motion communicates exactly one of five things.**

Intention. Effort. Arrival. Patience. Response. A motion that cannot name
which of the five it speaks is decoration, and decoration is cut. (Absorbed
from the Vision; elevated here to natural law.)

**§30. The rhythm of the world is many-then-one.**

Accumulation resolving into a single clear event: many embers, one scout; a
whole river, one landing; a full build, one crossing; a full minute, one
glance. The crescendo of the many exists to purchase the clarity of the one.
This rhythm is the Claradix story told as pure tempo — many small acts, one
reality — and it recurs at every scale, always.

## Article X — The Visitor and the Single Glance

**§31. The visitor is the shore.**

The bottom of the frame is where the visitor stands — their ground, their end
of the valley. The world treats it so: the build comes *toward* it, the
crossing arrives *at* it, the words land *on* it. The visitor is not watching
the story; they are its destination. This is how the world makes the visitor
part of the transformation without a single button: structurally, everything
that matters ends where they are.

**§32. The world notices the visitor exactly once.**

One moment per visit. Never twice. The Single Glance: a lean of light so
slight it could be imagined, toward where the visitor's attention rests, at a
moment when they have stopped expecting anything — and then the world returns
to its own life as if nothing happened.

The Glance is governed by three absolute conditions:

- **It is deniable.** It must always be possible to believe it was a
  coincidence. The moment it is unmistakable, it is a feature, and a feature
  cannot haunt anyone.
- **It is unrepeatable.** If the visitor tries to summon it again — waits for
  it, waves at it, repeats the conditions — nothing happens. Ever. A glance
  that can be farmed is an animation.
- **It is unexplained.** No documentation visible to visitors, no marketing
  copy, no tooltip, no confession, ever names it. Its entire value is the
  question it leaves behind: *did the world just notice me?*

The visitor who catches it will remember this page for years. The visitor who
misses it loses nothing. Both outcomes are correct.

**§33. The world never begs.**

Beyond the Glance, the world issues no instructions, no prompts, no pulsing
affordances, no tutorials. It is patient the way a place is patient. The
visitor who never touches anything has still been given a complete experience;
the visitor who touches is answered generously and instantly — but the asking
happens once, wordlessly, and never again.

**§34. The page is never hostage.**

The words never hide from someone who came only for the words. Whatever film
is playing, whatever miracle is mid-arc, a visitor who wants the practical
page must be able to have it without punishment, delay, or guilt. The
experience is a gift, and gifts are not mandatory.

## Article XI — Equilibrium

**§35. Completion is not an ending. It is the world reaching equilibrium.**

When the crossing lands and the words settle, the story does not stop — it
returns to the tempo of the world. The mist keeps its tides. The stars keep
their breath. The bridge, now part of the landscape, breathes at the pace of
built things. The valley is permanently different — lit low by what stands in
it — and permanently itself. Nothing is "idle." Idle is a machine waiting for
input; equilibrium is a place living at its own pace. The difference is
absolute: **an idle scene has stopped meaning; a world in equilibrium is
meaning slowly.**

**§36. The visitor arrived in the middle of an infinite story.**

The valley was worked before (§16) and will be worked again. Tonight's bridge
is one night of an endless practice — the newest thing ever made in a place
where making is old. The build the visitor watched was not staged for their
arrival; it was tonight's work, and they came early enough to see it. What
makes tonight *theirs* is only its direction: this crossing came to the shore
where they happened to be standing. The world owes no one a beginning and
promises no one an ending; it grants each visitor a middle, and the middle is
where all real stories are entered.

**§37. Equilibrium contains life and excludes performance.**

Permitted in equilibrium, forever: breath, tide, drift, the sky's slow change,
the bridge's sub-visible respiration, the world's watchfulness. Excluded,
forever: loops the eye can learn, events that repeat on any schedule,
crescendos without cause, anything that exists to re-capture attention. The
world after completion must be interesting the way a night landscape is
interesting — inexhaustibly, and without ever once trying.

## Article XII — The Signature

*What makes one unmarked second of this world recognizable as Claradix.*

**§38. The identity is behavioural, not ornamental.**

Claradix is not recognized by a logo placed on a scene. It is recognized the
way a composer is recognized: by how things move, gather, rest and answer.
Remove every mark and wordmark, show three seconds of this world to someone
who has seen it once before, and they should know where they are. This is the
**Unmarked Test**, and every future artefact of the brand must pass it.

**§39. The Shape: the Claradix Curve.**

The falling line that holds — the curve of something at rest under load. It is
the drape of the main cable, caught at the instant it settles. It is the arc
of every light's flight, rising and easing home. It is the profile of the
valley itself — the same curve carved downward by the old river that the cable
hangs upward in the air. The land and the bridge share one geometry, mirrored;
the oldest thing in the world and the newest thing in the world draw the same
line. That curve — tension resolved into grace, effort that has found its
form — is the mark of Claradix, whether drawn in light, in stone, on a page or
on a card.

**§40. The Movement: rise–travel–settle, decisive out and gentle in.**

(§19, §28.) Anywhere a Claradix thing moves — a particle, a word arriving, an
interface answering, a future product's smallest transition — it moves in
three acts, departs with intention and arrives with care, and returns to
stillness. This is the brand's gait. It is recognizable in a single gesture.

**§41. The Rhythm: many-then-one, with held breaths.**

(§30.) Accumulation, a stillness, a single clear event. And within any
composed sequence: pauses placed like punctuation — few, deliberate,
unrepeated. The signature rhythm of Claradix is the confidence to stop.

**§42. The Light: pooling, sourced, accumulated.**

(§5, §6.) Claradix light gathers and rests. It never beams, never flares,
never arrives from nowhere. In any future medium — film, interface, print
analogue — brightness means accumulated care, and light without a findable
source is off-brand at the level of physics.

**§43. The Crowd: skilled workers, never magic dust.**

(§18.) Wherever many small things move together under the Claradix name, they
move like a practiced crew: intentional, unhurried, individually alive,
collectively ordered, never chaotic and never crystalline. The many are always
in the state between — organic order. That state *is* the brand's texture.

## Article XIII — Supremacy

**§44.** Every implementation decision — every animation curve, every lighting
value, every camera placement, every interaction, every sound — is downstream
of this document. An implementation that contradicts a law here is not a
creative variation; it is a depiction of some other world, made by mistake.

**§45.** When a law and a beautiful idea conflict, the law wins — and the
beautiful idea is recorded in `DESIGN_DECISION_LOG.md` as a rejected
alternative, with the law that rejected it. The log of what this world refused
is part of what this world is.

**§46.** This constitution describes the world as it is. Five years from now,
the answers here must still be true — not because no one was allowed to change
them, but because they were true.

*Ratified as the foundation of the Claradix experience. All prior intent is
subordinate to it. The next thing built in this world obeys it.*
